## Known limitations

Stated before any result exists, and not a reason to change anything later.

- **`wardbeck` gap:** the fallback gap of §4.3 applies (D58), so only 6 h of its evaluation period come before the first attack, against 24 h on the other testbeds. There is less time to measure false positives before the attack.
- **`harrison` first phase:** the published `network_scans` row starts at 07:05:00, 120 s before the first attacker step in `attacks.log`, so the cross-check of the inventory fails on that row. The row stays as published (D60). No labeled line of an attack phase falls in those 120 s.
- **`harrison` volume:** `dirb` alone holds 411,830 labeled lines in 26 minutes, so attack lines are most of the parsed lines of the evaluation period, and the alert rate is far above the other testbeds. Line shares and the alert reduction are not comparable across testbeds.
- **Attack order:** exfiltration stops before the scans on santos and `wardbeck`. On `harrison`, as on russellmitchell, it runs through the attack, and `service_stop` comes last.
- **Unlabeled exfiltration:** `inet-dns` holds 45,254 such lines on `wardbeck` and 70,773 on `harrison`. The label-ambiguous rule of `reports/targets.md` handles them.

## What the counts can and cannot say

**Measured here, from window counts:** fit and calib windows, whether D28 adds out-of-fold (OOF) scores, the reference size `N`, the rank step `1/(3N)`, and the rank steps a window may lose at `q`. `q` means exactly 1.0 when `N` is under 334.

**UNVERIFIED, for `wardbeck` and `harrison`:** the ceiling itself. The ceiling of `reports/audit_incident_count.md` is `1 - k/(3N)`, where `k` counts the reference scores above the top score a model gives any window, summed over the three models. `k` needs saved scores, and no detection ran here. So counts cannot show that a log type is out of reach of `q`, and none is declared so. A type with no OOF score in its reference has no D28 cap, so its ceiling is 1. For a type with OOF scores, the `ceiling` column gives the highest value that already misses `q`, at the smallest `k` that does.

**Measured, for santos and russellmitchell:** the audit read `k` from saved scores, so the `UNVERIFIED` cells of those two sections have a measured answer (Q4). Russellmitchell has ceiling 1 on every log type. Santos has `apache_access` 0.999826, `audit` 0.999712, `dns` 1, and `auth` 0.998848 and `vpn` 0.992063, the two under `q`.
